# Pose Tracking in Humanoid MPC (Unitree G1)

This note summarizes how **pose tracking** is implemented in the G1 centroidal and whole‑body MPC stacks, and how different poses are tracked via **state space**, **joint space**, and **task space** costs.

Relevant configs:

- Centroidal MPC: `robot_models/unitree_g1/g1_centroidal_mpc/config/mpc/task.info`
- Whole‑body MPC: `robot_models/unitree_g1/g1_wb_mpc/config/mpc/task.info`

Core code:

- Centroidal interface: `humanoid_nmpc/humanoid_centroidal_mpc/src/CentroidalMpcInterface.cpp`
- Whole‑body interface: `humanoid_nmpc/humanoid_wb_mpc/src/WBMpcInterface.cpp`
- Shared cost/constraint factory: `humanoid_nmpc/humanoid_common_mpc/include/humanoid_common_mpc/HumanoidCostConstraintFactory.h`

---

## Common Quadratic State–Input Tracking

Both MPC problems use a standard quadratic tracking cost of the form

$$
  J = \int_{t_0}^{t_f} \underbrace{\| x(t) - x^\text{ref}(t) \|_{Q}^2}_{\text{state tracking}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\| u(t) - u^\text{ref}(t) \|_{R}^2 }_{\text{input tracking}}\; dt
  \;+ \| x(t_f) - x^\text{ref}(t_f) \|_{Q_f}^2.
$$

The weights `Q`, `R`, `Q_final` are configured in each `task.info`:

- Centroidal: state index layout is centroidal momentum, base pose, and MPC‑active joint angles (`g1_centroidal_mpc/config/mpc/task.info`).
- Whole‑body: state layout is base pose, joint positions, base velocities, and joint velocities (`g1_wb_mpc/config/mpc/task.info`).

This captures:

- **Base pose tracking** (torso position/orientation) via the base entries of `Q` and `Q_final`.
- **Joint‑space tracking** of leg/torso/arm joint angles and velocities via the joint entries of `Q` and `Q_final`.

In other words, even without explicit task‑space costs, torso and arm poses are indirectly tracked through appropriate joint‑space penalties.

## Centroidal MPC Pose Tracking

### State‑Space Tracking (Centroidal Model)

State layout (from `docs/g1_centroidal_mpc.md`):

$$
  x = \begin{bmatrix}
    \bar{\mathbf{h}} \\
    \mathbf{p}_b \\
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_{b,zyx} \\
    \mathbf{q}_j
  \end{bmatrix}
  \quad\text{with}\quad
  \bar{\mathbf{h}} = \frac{1}{m}
  \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{p}_\text{lin} \\ \mathbf{l}\end{bmatrix},
$$

where:

- $\mathbf{p}_b \in \mathbb{R}^3$: base position,
- $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{b,zyx}$: ZYX Euler angles (base orientation),
- $\mathbf{q}_j$: active joint angles for the MPC model.

The quadratic term in `Q` penalizes:

- Deviation of centroidal momentum $\bar{\mathbf{h}}$ from its reference.
- Base pose deviation $(\mathbf{p}_b, \boldsymbol{\theta}_{b,zyx})$.
- Joint configuration deviation $\mathbf{q}_j$.

Effectively, centroidal MPC tracks torso pose and joint posture in **state space**.

### Foot Task‑Space Tracking (Centroidal)

In `CentroidalMpcInterface::setupOptimalControlProblem`:

- Foot‑space tracking costs are added via `HumanoidCostConstraintFactory`:
  - A base set of weights for feet: `task_space_foot_cost_weights.` in `g1_centroidal_mpc/config/mpc/task.info`.
  - Additional link costs in the optional `task_space_costs` block, where each entry specifies:
    - `link_name` (Pinocchio frame),
    - Weights for position/orientation in `task_space_costs.<name>.weights.`.

Conceptually, for a link $k$ (e.g. foot), the cost term per time is:

$$
  \ell_\text{ts}(t) =
  \sum_k
  \Big(
    w_p \,\|\mathbf{p}_k(x(t)) - \mathbf{p}_k^\text{ref}(t)\|^2
    +
    w_R \,\text{dist}_R(R_k(x(t)), R_k^\text{ref}(t))^2
  \Big),
$$

where:

- $\mathbf{p}_k(x)$ is the position of frame $k$,
- $R_k(x)$ is its orientation,
- $\text{dist}_R$ is a suitable orientation error metric (e.g. axis-angle or quaternion error),
- $w_p$, $w_R$ come from the configured weights.

This allows centroidal MPC to track:

- Feet in task space (position/orientation).
- Any extra links listed under `task_space_costs` (e.g. torso, head, hands) purely by configuration.

## Whole‑Body MPC Pose Tracking

### State & Joint‑Space Tracking (Whole‑Body)

Whole‑body MPC uses a full joint‑space state:

$$
  x =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{p}_b \\
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_{b,zyx} \\
    \mathbf{q}_j \\
    \mathbf{v}_b \\
    \boldsymbol{\omega}_b \\
    \dot{\mathbf{q}}_j
  \end{bmatrix},
$$

with weights `Q` and `Q_final` in `g1_wb_mpc/config/mpc/task.info` that:

- Track base pose $(\mathbf{p}_b, \boldsymbol{\theta}_{b,zyx})$.
- Track joint positions $\mathbf{q}_j$ for legs, torso, and arms.
- Track base and joint velocities.

The same quadratic form applies:

$$
  \ell_\text{state}(t) = \| x(t) - x^\text{ref}(t) \|_Q^2,
$$

with $x^\text{ref}(t)$ generated by the `TargetTrajectories` and modified by the reference manager (e.g. adding arm swing).

### Foot Task‑Space Tracking (Whole‑Body)

In `WBMpcInterface::setupOptimalControlProblem`:

- For each contact frame `footName` in `modelSettings_.contactNames[i]`:

  1. An end‑effector dynamics model $f_k$ is built:

     ```cpp
     eeDynamicsPtr.reset(new PinocchioEndEffectorDynamicsCppAd(
         *pinocchioInterfacePtr_, *mpcRobotModelADPtr_, {footName}, footName,
         modelSettings_.modelFolderCppAd,
         modelSettings_.recompileLibrariesCppAd,
         modelSettings_.verboseCppAd));
     ```

  2. Constraint terms enforce stance behavior when the foot is in contact:
     - Friction cone, contact moment, zero wrench, zero velocity, normal velocity, etc.

  3. A **task‑space tracking cost** is added:

     ```cpp
     EndEffectorDynamicsWeights footTrackingCostWeights =
         EndEffectorDynamicsWeights::getWeights(taskFile_, "task_space_foot_cost_weights.", verbose_);

     std::string footTrackingCostName = footName + "_TaskSpaceTrackingCost";

     problemPtr_->costPtr->add(
       footTrackingCostName,
       std::unique_ptr<StateInputCost>(new EndEffectorDynamicsFootCost(
         *referenceManagerPtr_, footTrackingCostWeights, *pinocchioInterfacePtr_,
         *eeDynamicsPtr, *mpcRobotModelADPtr_, i, footTrackingCostName, modelSettings_)));
     ```

- The weights `task_space_foot_cost_weights` in `g1_wb_mpc/config/mpc/task.info` specify penalties on:
  - Position, orientation,
  - Linear and angular velocities,
  - Linear and angular accelerations in the foot frame.

Conceptually, for each foot end‑effector $k$, the cost includes terms like:

$$
  \ell_\text{foot}(t) =
  \sum_{k \in \{\text{feet}\}}
  \Big(
    w_p \,\|\mathbf{p}_k(x(t)) - \mathbf{p}_k^\text{ref}(t)\|^2
    +
    w_R \,\text{dist}_R(R_k(x(t)), R_k^\text{ref}(t))^2
    +
    w_v \,\|\mathbf{v}_k(x,u) - \mathbf{v}_k^\text{ref}(t)\|^2
    +
    w_a \,\|\mathbf{a}_k(x,u) - \mathbf{a}_k^\text{ref}(t)\|^2
  \Big),
$$

where:

- $\mathbf{p}_k, R_k$ come from Pinocchio kinematics.
- $\mathbf{v}_k, \mathbf{a}_k$ come from the end‑effector dynamics model $f_k$.

The reference manager (`SwitchedModelReferenceManager`) provides:

- Foot target trajectories (via `SwingTrajectoryPlanner` and the gait schedule).
- Arm‑swing‑modified joint references (which influence the state tracking cost).

## Adding Additional Task‑Space Pose Tracking (e.g. Hands)

From this structure, adding task‑space tracking for extra links (e.g., wrists as end effectors) follows the same pattern as the feet:

1. **Choose link frames**:
   - Identify the desired end‑effector frames in the URDF (e.g. `left_wrist_yaw_link`, `right_wrist_yaw_link` or custom hand frames).

2. **Define weights in the task file**:
   - For centroidal MPC:
     - Add entries under `task_space_costs` specifying:
       - `link_name`,
       - Weight block `task_space_costs.<name>.weights.` with position/orientation weights.
   - For whole‑body MPC:
     - Add a new weight block similar to `task_space_foot_cost_weights` (or reuse that structure) for the hand links.

3. **Wire in the cost in the interface**:
   - Centroidal:
     - `CentroidalMpcInterface::setupOptimalControlProblem` already loops over `task_space_costs` entries and builds generic end‑effector costs for them. Adding entries in the config is often enough.
   - Whole‑body:
     - Extend `WBMpcInterface::setupOptimalControlProblem` to:
       - Instantiate `PinocchioEndEffectorDynamicsCppAd` (or `EndEffectorKinematics`) for the wrist frames.
       - Add additional `StateInputCost` instances (similar to `EndEffectorDynamicsFootCost`) that use their own weight sets and reference trajectories.

4. **Provide reference trajectories**:
   - Either:
     - Derive from existing joint‑space references (e.g., convert desired arm joint patterns to task‑space pose), or
     - Specify explicit trajectories in the `reference.info` and have the reference manager expose them.

In summary:

- **Centroidal MPC** tracks torso and joints via the centroidal state, with optional task‑space costs for specific frames (feet and extra links) configured in the task file.
- **Whole‑body MPC** tracks torso and arms via joint‑space weights and arm‑swing references, and feet explicitly in task space (position/orientation/vel/acc) using end‑effector dynamics costs.
- Extending pose tracking to additional end effectors (like wrists) is primarily a matter of:
  - Selecting frames,
  - Defining appropriate weights in `task.info`,
  - Wiring new end‑effector cost terms in the MPC interface.
